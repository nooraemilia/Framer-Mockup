Navigating a website shouldn't feel like solving a puzzle, yet ILGA Portugal's site created barriers for the very people it exists to support. This project reimagined the site so anyone seeking information, events, or help could find it quickly and confidently.

It began with a core question: How do you design a digital space that truly serves a marginalized community with clarity, warmth, and care?

ILGA Portugal has been the country's leading LGBTQIA+ advocacy organization since 1995, grounded in three pillars: political advocacy, support services, and community empowerment. Yet usability testing revealed that many first-time visitors struggled to understand ILGA's mission within seconds of landing on the homepage.

Over six weeks, I conducted stakeholder interviews, competitive benchmarking, accessibility research, and two rounds of usability testing — leading the full process from research through high-fidelity prototyping as a solo designer.

Challenge

The existing website struggled with information clarity and structural hierarchy. In usability testing with six participants:

67%
had never heard of ILGA before
50%
read the homepage as an employment agency
83%
struggled to find events
50%
couldn't locate resources

Participants included LGBTQIA+ community members, culturally informed allies, a user with hearing impairment, and neurodivergent participants — most of them immigrants living in Portugal, reflecting real first-time scenarios. Key pain points:

  • No clearly labeled "Events" section, despite strong user expectation
  • Confusion between "Activities," "Projects," and "Programs"
  • Resources scattered across unclear categories
  • Footer preferred over the hamburger menu for visibility
  • Accessibility gaps: low contrast and no font resizing

"Many people don't immediately understand what ILGA does. Our critical work is buried, and users rely on Google to find our own pages."

— Stakeholder interview
Solution

Through stakeholder interviews, competitive analysis of five NGOs, a Nielsen heuristics evaluation, and WCAG research, one principle became clear: the site had to reflect user mental models, not internal terminology. I rebuilt the information architecture around five user-centered sections:

  • Homepage — communicates ILGA's mission and three pillars within seconds
  • Events — a dedicated hub for community gatherings and campaigns
  • Knowledge Hub — one place for resources, research, and policy
  • Get Involved — clear pathways for volunteering and advocacy
  • Support — direct access to legal, mental-health, and community services

In a second round of testing with the redesign, results shifted sharply:

100%
completed tasks independently
0
confusion around events or homepage purpose
2
rounds of testing, refined each time

Accessibility ran through the whole redesign: improved color contrast while keeping brand identity, larger action-oriented buttons, a clearer typographic hierarchy, and consideration for dark mode, font resizing, and captioned media.

Although not yet implemented, the redesign demonstrated measurable improvement in task success, brand clarity, and navigation efficiency — and I remain in contact with ILGA leadership about bringing it to life.
